Learn Data Access Control with Phoebe

Access goes wrong in three directions at once and most teams only count one of them. Somebody holds a column they have no business seeing. Somebody else cannot do the job they were hired for, so the data reaches them through a shared spreadsheet with no logging at all. And a third pile of grants sits there needed by nobody, waiting for one compromised account to make it interesting. This course builds the machinery that handles all three: role hierarchies, row access policies, column masking with honest exception roles, tokenisation, secrets, and an access review that a pipeline produces rather than a person. Session 8 scores the finished set against 150 concrete access requests, and then shows what a single ACCOUNTADMIN grant does to it.

What is real here 🧾

A course about proving who can see what should be exact about its own claims.

ClaimStatus
The 150 access requestsEvery combination of 5 personas, 10 columns and 3 regions. Of those, 41 legitimately need a value in clear, 3 need it masked and 106 have no business being answered
The ground truthA written access design: per job, which columns are needed in clear, which masked, and which regions the job covers. Visible in the widget, not hidden in an answer key
The evaluationA real policy engine. Row policies are applied before masking policies, column grants come from the role split, masking transforms what the grants allowed. All three counters are counted from what came back
The ladder: 109, 94, 70, 22, 3, 0 leaksComputed in the browser. The step from 70 to 22 is the role split, which also takes standing privileges from 17 to 1
Strip every PII column: 0 leaks, 8 over-blocksArithmetic. All 8 are the support agent, across name, email, phone and support_notes in both regions they cover
Grant ACCOUNTADMIN: 109 leaks, 30 standing privilegesArithmetic, and identical to the opening state. The policies are still defined and deployed; a role above the policy layer is not constrained by them
Nothing on the bench is modelledUnlike a simulator, there is no human behaviour to estimate here. Every number is a count of policy outcomes against a written design
Snowflake role and policy behaviourRead from the vendor documentation: the system roles and their purposes, ownership and managed access schemas, primary versus secondary roles with the CREATE exception, and that a row access policy is evaluated before any masking policy on the same object
RBAC compared with ABACFramed per NIST SP 800-162, which defines ABAC as evaluating attributes of subject, object, operation and environment against policy rather than assigning static roles
SaltboxA fictional consumer lending startup. The failure patterns are ordinary ones; the company is not real
